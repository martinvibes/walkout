Why they left

The numbers say who left; the film says what they were watching. Put the two together and the fix has an owner — an editor, a translator, or an engineer.

The agent

Works through every cliff on its own: pulls the numbers, watches the film, and writes up what to fix and what it would win back. About half a minute.

Method

Two witnesses that never spoke to each other.

Your numbers know exactly when people left but have never seen the film. A model that watches the film has no idea which thirty seconds out of ninety minutes matter. Walkout is the conversation between them.

01

Find where they left

We read every play, pause and quit from every viewer and look for the moments where far more people stopped than this title normally loses. Every film has a slow leak; we are only interested in the cliffs. Someone who watched to the end is not counted as having left.

02

Work out who left

The same moment gets cut by phone, TV, app version, country, language and delivery network. If everyone quit at the same rate, the problem is on screen. If it is mostly one group, the problem is whatever that group had in common.

03

Watch the moment

Gemini watches those exact seconds of the real film and describes what is happening — how it is paced, whether anyone is speaking, whether the picture itself looks broken. It is never told that anyone walked out there, so when it independently flags the same moment, that is real corroboration.

04

Say what to fix, and to whom

Buffering on one app build is an engineering ticket, and Walkout will tell you in as many words not to touch the edit. Viewers in one language with no subtitle track is a job for your localization team. A quiet scene everyone walked out of is a note for the editor. When it genuinely cannot tell, it says so instead of guessing.

Walkout runs on the playback telemetry a streaming service already collects — the same events behind any retention chart. This demo runs on Sintel (Blender Foundation, CC-BY) with 13 million simulated viewing events, and cliffs planted at known timecodes for known reasons so the diagnosis can be graded rather than merely admired. The film itself is watched straight from YouTube; it is never copied or hosted here.
